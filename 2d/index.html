Getting Started

Up and running in 60 seconds

No installation wizard, no dependencies. Download, extract, run.

1

Download

Click the download button for your operating system above. The file comes as a compressed archive.

2

Extract

Unzip the folder anywhere on your computer. Windows users: right-click → Extract All. Linux users: tar -xf file or use your file manager.

3

Run

Open the extracted folder and double-click the sprite_editor executable. No install required. On Linux you may need to right-click → Properties → Permissions → Allow executing.

4

Start Drawing

The editor opens with a blank canvas and a sample character loaded. Pick a tool on the right, pick a color, and click on the canvas to paint.

Tools

Every tool you need. Nothing you don't.

Eleven focused tools — each one designed for pixel-perfect work. Hover any icon in the editor to see its shortcut.

Brush B

Paint pixels with 4 effects: Normal, Spray, Glow, and Texture. Size 1–16px, adjustable opacity.

Eraser E

Erase pixels back to transparent. Right-click with any tool to erase temporarily.

Fill / Bucket

Flood-fill connected pixels of a similar color. Tolerance slider lets you control how picky it is.

Magic Wand W

Click a color to erase all connected pixels of that color. Fast way to clean up backgrounds.

Color Picker I

Click any pixel on the canvas to grab its color as your active color.

Rectangle Select M

Drag to select a rectangular area. All paint tools then stay inside that box.

Ellipse Select O

Same as Rectangle, but selects an ellipse. Great for circular sprites.

Lasso Select L

Click points to draw a custom selection polygon. Click near the first point to close it.

Path Brush P

Click to place points, right-click to apply the stroke. 12 styles: smooth, dotted, dashed, zigzag, wavy, ribbon, and more.

Move V

Move pixels inside the selection, or move the whole frame with arrow keys.

Text T

Stamp uppercase letters and numbers on the canvas using a built-in 5×7 pixel font.

Blender-style Transforms

Rotate. Grab. Scale. Chain them freely.

Select an area, then press R, G, or S. Move your mouse to transform. Left-click to apply, right-click or Esc to cancel. Switch between transforms at any time — they stack.

R

Rotate

Spin the selection around its center

  • Angles snap to 45° increments
  • Axis is fixed at the center of your selection
  • Press G afterward to move the rotated result
G

Grab

Move the selection freely

  • Pixels keep their original color
  • Pixels outside the canvas are preserved until you commit
  • Cancel and restore at any time with Esc
S

Scale

Shrink or expand from the center

  • Move mouse away from center to grow
  • Move mouse toward center to shrink
  • Nearest-neighbor keeps pixel edges crisp
R → G → S → ✓ Apply Chain any combination — each step commits silently and the next one starts from there.

Add Frame

Click the green + button to add a new empty frame. The new frame starts hidden — click its eye icon to make it visible.

📋

Copy / Paste

Click COPY to store the current frame, then PASTE into any other frame (or other direction/animation). Perfect for making a variant.

🟡

Merge Frames

Shift+click to mark frames (they turn magenta), then press MERGE to combine them into one. Newer frames win on conflicts.

▶

Play / Preview

Click PLAY (or press Space) to preview the animation. Adjust FPS with the slider. Hidden frames are skipped during playback.

Capture & Trace

Trace any image into pixel art

Import a reference image, paint over it, then capture exactly what's inside the amber frame as a 64×64 sprite.

1

Open the Capture Modal

Click the amber CAPTURAR button in the top-right. A full-screen workspace opens.

2

Load a Reference Image

Click Load Image on the left sidebar. Pick any PNG or JPG. It appears as a semi-transparent layer.

3

Paint Over It

Use the same paint tools. Zoom with the mouse wheel, pan with right-drag. Lower the image opacity to see your pixels better.

4

Capture

Position the image so the part you want sits inside the amber square. Press F12 or click Capture → 2D. The 64×64 area becomes your current frame.
